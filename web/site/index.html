How this number is computed

The image is resized to 50×50 and shown to the model in 8 geometric views (rotations and flips). The reported probability is the mean of the 8 outputs; the interval is mean ± 1.96×SD of those 8 outputs. Wide intervals mean the model is unsure about that cell.

About malaria

Malaria is an acute febrile illness caused by Plasmodium parasites, which are spread to people through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. There are 5 parasite species that cause malaria in humans, and 2 of these species, P. falciparum and P. vivax, pose the greatest threat. P. falciparum is the deadliest malaria parasite and the most prevalent on the African continent. P. vivax is the dominant malaria parasite in most countries outside of sub-Saharan Africa.

How staging works

When a cell is flagged as infected, the pipeline outlines the parasite with contour detection and measures the ratio of parasite area to cell area. That ratio places the infection in one of three stages:

Parasite-to-cell area ratioStage
7% and aboveAdvanced schizont stage
2.5% to 7%Trophozoite stage
Below 2.5%Initial ring stage

Methodology from the original analysis pipeline: contrast adjustment and cleanup of the cell image, then OpenCV contour detection to outline the parasite and measure its area against the cell.

    About this model

    A compact CNN trained on the NIH malaria cell image dataset (27,558 thin-blood-smear cell images, half parasitized, half uninfected). On the full 27,558-image NIH set it reaches 93.2% accuracy (95% CI 92.9–93.5), sensitivity 92.9%, specificity 93.5%, AUC 0.958. Mean TTA spread across 8 views is ±0.055 in probability. Full write-up, code, and figures: github.com/nepalanurag/Detection-of-Malaria-Using-CNN.

    Research demo, not a medical device. Predictions can be wrong; see the error analysis in the repo before trusting it.
